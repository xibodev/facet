# Review And Editing Guidance

Apply this guidance after the source-edit method has established the editorial
promise and inspected the source.

## Edit

- Open with the strongest moment that honestly serves the request; remove
  repetition, dead time, accidental starts or stops, and distractions.
- Preserve intelligible speech, action continuity, eyelines, and enough context
  for each cut to read. Use effects and transitions only when they communicate
  structure or conceal an unavoidable discontinuity.
- Reframe intentionally. Check faces, hands, text, product details, and motion at
  representative points rather than assuming one crop fits an entire shot.
- Keep useful source sound. Balance speech first; use supplied music only with a
  clear purpose and known permission, and avoid masking words or abrupt audio
  boundaries.

## Review

Separate mechanical evidence from editorial judgment. Tool success means the
operation completed, not that the video is acceptable.

- Verify the requested dimensions, frame rate, duration tolerance, codecs,
  playable streams, audio presence, and output location from tool evidence.
- Watch the complete render with sound. Check the opening, ending, every cut,
  crop and transition, speech clarity, sync, pacing, continuity, visual defects,
  silence, clipping, and music balance against the editorial promise.
- Inspect sampled frames for framing and corruption, but never substitute frame
  samples for watching motion and listening to audio.
- Revise the smallest responsible input: edit decisions for story or pacing,
  framing for crop defects, and mix settings for audio defects. Recheck the
  changed region and rerun final technical checks after rendering.
- Record `pass`, `warn`, or `fail` with concise evidence. Deliver warnings only
  when the output remains useful and the limitation is made explicit; do not
  deliver a known failed render as complete.
