# Algae — 30s Explainer

**Audience:** general / curious adult. **Aspect:** 16:9 1920x1080 @30fps. **Tone:** wonder, grounded in fact.

## Narrative arc
hook (breath) -> reframe (not trees) -> scale (50% of oxygen) -> mechanism (no roots/leaves)
-> speed (doubles in a day) -> so-what (fuel/food/carbon) -> landing (smallest doing biggest)

## Narration (VO)

Take a breath. Now take another. One of those came from algae.
Not from a forest — from something you can barely see.
Algae make about half the oxygen on Earth.
No roots. No leaves. Just sunlight, water, and carbon dioxide.
And they're fast — some double their mass in a single day.
Which is why we're turning them into fuel, food, and a way to pull carbon out of the sky.
The smallest thing in the ocean, doing the biggest job.

## Fact grounding
- "about half the oxygen" — marine phytoplankton are commonly credited with ~50% of
  net primary oxygen production. Hedged with "about" deliberately; estimates range 50-80%.
- "double their mass in a single day" — true of several fast-growing microalgae
  (e.g. Chlorella) under favourable conditions. Hedged with "some".
- Algae are not plants; they lack true roots/leaves/stems. Stated as mechanism, not taxonomy.
